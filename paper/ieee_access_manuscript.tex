\begin{abstract}
Machine-learning-based intrusion detection systems (IDS) in Internet of Things (IoT) edge environments face three critical operational bottlenecks: (1) static decision boundaries that explode in false alarms under non-stationary traffic rate drift; (2) catastrophic threshold collapse or poisoning under sustained attack bursts; and (3) severe alert fatigue caused by tens of thousands of isolated, unranked flow-level alerts. In recent literature, Saeed (IEEE Access, 2025) proposed integrating LSTM anomaly detection, Q-learning mitigation, and lattice-based cryptography, but relied on fixed percentile thresholding, reported no global classification metrics across a full test stream, and explicitly cited dynamic thresholding and alert handling as vital open challenges. 

This paper presents DACC-ID, a drift-aware, contextual cybersecurity incident detection and correlation framework evaluated on 100,000 held-out flows of the public CICIoT2023 benchmark across 5 standardized random seeds under a strict Test-Label Firewall. Our primary empirical contributions establish that:
(1) Sample-level Dense Autoencoders ($L=1$) eliminate sequence boundary distortion on tabular NetFlow, matching recurrent LSTM accuracy (F1 = $88.54\% \pm 0.13\%$) while using $12.0\times$ fewer parameters (10,775 vs. 129,255) and running $3.67\times$ faster ($1.35\,\mu\text{s}$ vs. $4.95\,\mu\text{s}$);
(2) Naive adaptive methods suffer severe failure modes: Raw EWMA experiences an upward inflation collapse ($2,403,808\times$ threshold explosion, dropping recall to $0.03\%$), whereas Excluded EWMA suffers downward censoring collapse due to truncation bias (inflating nominal false alarms to $13.60\%$);
(3) Our proposed Truncation-Bias Corrected Adaptive (TCA) controller dynamically stabilizes the decision boundary via validation-calibrated factor $\kappa$ and streak-based incident freezing, slashing false alarms under $2.0\times$ feature rate drift by up to $77\%$ (FPR reduced from $27.33\%$ to $8.25\%$) while retaining $79.18\%$ recall under 20,000-sample attack floods;
(4) Our hybrid detection engine fuses supervised Random Forest attack classification ($p_t$) with unsupervised anomaly scoring ($a_t$), achieving F1 = $92.53\%$ and nominal FPR = $4.77\%$;
(5) The temporal incident correlation graph engine groups 74,453 raw flow alerts into 91 coherent incidents, achieving an Alert Reduction Rate (ARR) of $99.88\%$ with $100\%$ incident-level precision, reconstructing 90 multi-stage MITRE ATT\&CK campaigns and prioritizing them into actionable SOC triage tiers;
(6) Defensive Deep Q-Networks achieve $71.26\% \pm 0.85\%$ attack containment under un-contaminated evaluation ($0.63\%$ false block rate), while standardized NIST FIPS 203 ML-KEM-768 key encapsulation ($0.429\,\text{ms}$) and tamper-evident hash chaining ($19,379\,\text{records/s}$) guarantee quantum-resilient confidentiality and verifiable forensic integrity.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
The rapid proliferation of Internet of Things (IoT) devices in smart infrastructure, industrial automation, and healthcare ecosystems has expanded the cyber-attack surface \cite{alcaraz2023,kumar2019}. Heterogeneous edge nodes frequently lack hardware-level cryptographic isolation, rendering them targets for automated botnets (e.g., Mirai), distributed denial-of-service (DDoS) floods, and multi-stage lateral infiltration \cite{bertino2017,kolias2017}. Traditional rule-based intrusion detection systems (IDS) relying on static signature matching fail against polymorphic variants and zero-day threats \cite{roesch1999}.

To overcome these constraints, recent literature has explored multi-layer AI-driven architectures. Notably, Saeed \cite{saeed2025} proposed integrating Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) autoencoders for anomaly detection, Q-learning for automated threat mitigation, and theoretical Learning With Errors (LWE) lattice cryptography for post-quantum confidentiality. While conceptually broad, Saeed's study exhibited three critical limitations:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Absence of Global Stream Evaluation}: Saeed reported results primarily through a 4-row illustrative sample (timestamps 800--815) and localized ROC curves, providing no global Precision, Recall, F1-Score, or False Positive Rate (FPR) across an entire held-out test stream.
    \item \textbf{Static Threshold Brittleness}: The decision boundary was fixed at the 95th percentile of validation errors. As Saeed explicitly acknowledged: \textit{"while the model effectively identifies the presence of an anomaly, it underestimates the magnitude of abrupt spikes... Future improvements could include dynamic thresholding, ensemble models, or hybrid approaches... These strategies were not implemented in the present work"} \cite{saeed2025}. Under non-stationary traffic surges, static thresholds trigger severe false alarm floods.
    \item \textbf{Alert Fatigue and Isolated Decisions}: Generating isolated binary decisions for individual flow records overwhelms human analysts in modern Security Operations Centers (SOCs). In a stream with $85\%$ attack density, tens of thousands of unranked alerts are emitted without temporal or causal correlation.
\end{enumerate}

To address these foundational gaps, this paper presents \textbf{DACC-ID} (Drift-Aware Contextual Cybersecurity Incident Detection and Correlation Framework). Rather than attempting to incrementally replace deep learning models, DACC-ID addresses the end-to-end operational lifecycle of cybersecurity monitoring:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Empirical Model Dissection}: We demonstrate that on tabular NetFlow features, recurrent sequence models (LSTM-AE, GRU-AE) introduce sequence-boundary distortion without improving accuracy. A sample-level Dense Autoencoder ($L=1$) achieves equivalent detection ($88.54\%$ F1) with $12\times$ fewer parameters and $3.67\times$ lower latency.
    \item \textbf{Mathematically Rigorous Adaptive Thresholding}: We analyze the dual failure modes of online thresholding---\textit{Upward Inflation Collapse} in raw EWMA and \textit{Downward Censoring Collapse} in excluded EWMA---and propose the Truncation-Bias Corrected Adaptive (TCA) controller.
    \item \textbf{Incident Correlation & Alert Fatigue Reduction}: We implement a streaming graph-based correlation engine that collapses 74,453 raw flow alerts into 91 actionable incidents (a $99.88\%$ Alert Reduction Rate), prioritizing them into a 4-tier operational risk model mapped to MITRE ATT\&CK tactics.
    \item \textbf{Standardized Post-Quantum Defense}: We replace abstract lattice assumptions with concrete NIST FIPS 203 ML-KEM-768 key encapsulation and append-only cryptographic hash chaining.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Pre-Registered Data Partitioning & Test-Label Firewall}
All experiments are conducted on the public CICIoT2023 dataset \cite{ciciot2023}, consisting of 33 attack subclasses and pure benign telemetry. We extract 39 statistical flow features, discarding metadata identifiers (`src\_ip`, `dst\_ip`, `flow\_id`, `Timestamp`) to prevent shortcut identity memorization.

To ensure scientific validity, the data is partitioned into four strictly isolated subsets:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $\mathcal{D}_{\text{train}}$ ($100,000$ benign flows): Fits feature scalers and trains unsupervised autoencoders.
    \item $\mathcal{D}_{\text{val}}$ ($30,000$ benign flows): Calibrates baseline threshold percentiles, truncation factors, and EWMA hyperparameters.
    \item $\mathcal{D}_{\text{sup}}$ ($40,000$ balanced benign/attack flows): Trains supervised Random Forest classifiers and reinforcement learning mitigation policies.
    \item $\mathcal{D}_{\text{test}}$ ($100,000$ flows: 14,911 benign, 85,089 attack): Dedicated held-out evaluation stream.
\end{itemize}
A strict \textbf{Test-Label Firewall} is enforced: ground-truth labels $y_t \in \{0, 1\}$ are never accessed during streaming inference, threshold updates, or alert correlation, and are used exclusively in offline metric aggregation.

\subsection{Pillar 1: Deep Anomaly Detection Modeling}
An input flow vector $x_t \in \mathbb{R}^{39}$ is processed by a symmetric Dense Autoencoder ($39 \to 24 \to 12 \to 24 \to 39$) parameterized by weights $\theta$:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{x}_t = g_{\theta}(f_{\theta}(x_t))
\end{equation}
The reconstruction error is measured via Mean Squared Error (MSE):
\begin{equation}
    E_t = \frac{1}{D} \sum_{j=1}^D (x_{t,j} - \hat{x}_{t,j})^2
\end{equation}
Whereas Saeed \cite{saeed2025} utilized recurrent LSTM units over sliding temporal windows ($k=30$), tabular flow records represent discrete connection summaries rather than uniformly sampled continuous physical signals. Imposing sequential windows over tabular NetFlow risks cross-flow boundary corruption.

\subsection{Pillar 2: Mathematical Dynamic Thresholding (TCA-Adaptive)}
Standard detection applies a fixed threshold $\tau_{95}$ calibrated as the 95th percentile of validation errors $\mathcal{E}_{\text{val}}$. Under non-stationary traffic, online adaptation is required.

\subsubsection{Failure Modes of Conventional Online Thresholding}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Raw EWMA (Upward Inflation Collapse)}:
    \begin{equation}
        \mu_t = (1 - \alpha)\mu_{t-1} + \alpha E_t, \quad \tau_t = \mu_t + k \sigma_t
    \end{equation}
    When an attack burst occurs ($E_t \gg \tau_t$), anomalous errors enter the running mean, inflating $\tau_t$ by orders of magnitude and causing complete detection blindness (Recall drops to $0.03\%$).
    \item \textbf{Excluded EWMA (Downward Censoring Collapse)}:
    Updating $\mu_t$ and $\sigma_t^2$ only when $E_t \le \tau_t$ prevents attack poisoning but introduces severe statistical truncation bias:
    \begin{equation}
        \mathbb{E}[E \mid E \le \tau] < \mathbb{E}[E], \quad \text{Var}(E \mid E \le \tau) \ll \text{Var}(E)
    \end{equation}
    The running mean and variance steadily decay, causing $\tau_t$ to drop by $\sim 90\%$ and inflating nominal false alarms to $13.60\%$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{The Proposed TCA-Adaptive Formulation}
Our Truncation-Bias Corrected Adaptive (TCA) controller resolves both failure modes:
1. \textbf{Truncation Correction Factor ($\kappa$)}:
During validation on clean traffic, we compute:
\begin{equation}
    \kappa = \frac{\tau_{\text{calib}}}{\mu_{\text{trunc}} + k \cdot \sigma_{\text{trunc}}}
\end{equation}
where $\mu_{\text{trunc}}$ and $\sigma_{\text{trunc}}$ are computed on $\mathcal{E}_{\text{val}} \le \tau_{\text{calib}}$. At runtime, the threshold is dynamically reconstructed as:
\begin{equation}
    \tau_t = \kappa \cdot (\mu_t + k \cdot \sigma_t)
\end{equation}
2. \textbf{Winsorized Updates}: Samples are bounded at $\min(E_t, \tau_{\text{calib}})$ prior to statistical update.
3. \textbf{Streak-Based Incident Freeze}: If alerts persist for $\ge 3$ consecutive samples, adaptation freezes until 20 consecutive benign samples pass, preventing threshold poisoning during sustained floods.
4. \textbf{Operational Envelope}: $\tau_t \in [\rho_{\min}\tau_{\text{calib}}, \rho_{\max}\tau_{\text{calib}}]$ ($[0.5, 2.5]\times$).

\subsection{Pillar 3: Hybrid Detection & ADWIN Drift Monitoring}
We formulate a calibrated hybrid detector fusing supervised Random Forest probability $p_t \in [0, 1]$ and normalized unsupervised anomaly score $a_t \in [0, 1]$:
\begin{equation}
    S_t = \alpha p_t + (1 - \alpha) a_t \quad (\alpha = 0.65)
\end{equation}
To detect non-stationary concept drift, we incorporate the \textbf{ADWIN (Adaptive Windowing)} algorithm. ADWIN maintains a variable-length sliding window $W$. When two subwindows $W_0, W_1$ exhibit mean score differences exceeding the Hoeffding bound:
\begin{equation}
    |\mu_{W_0} - \mu_{W_1}| > \epsilon_{\text{cut}} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{2m} \ln\left(\frac{4|W|}{\delta}\right)}
\end{equation}
where $m = 1/(1/|W_0| + 1/|W_1|)$, concept drift is confirmed and older subwindows are flushed.

\subsection{Pillar 4: Temporal Incident Correlation Graph & Contextual Prioritization}
Individual alerts $e_i = (t_i, d_i, ip_i, s_i, r_i)$ are clustered into coherent multi-stage incidents $\mathcal{I}_k$ using streaming temporal session correlation with an inactivity timeout $W_t = 45.0\,\text{s}$ and causal progression matching across MITRE ATT\&CK tactics (Reconnaissance $\to$ Credential Access $\to$ Exploitation $\to$ Denial of Service).

Each correlated incident $\mathcal{I}_k$ is assigned a contextual operational risk score $R_k \in [0, 1]$:
\begin{equation}
    R_k = w_1 D_k + w_2 I_k + w_3 C_k + w_4 E_k
\end{equation}
where $D_k$ is mean detection confidence, $I_k$ is target asset/service criticality, $C_k = 1 - \exp(-\lambda |\mathcal{I}_k|)$ is multi-event corroboration, and $E_k$ is event arrival density. Incidents are sorted into four triage priority tiers: `CRITICAL` ($R \ge 0.75$), `HIGH` ($0.55 \le R < 0.75$), `MEDIUM` ($0.35 \le R < 0.55$), and `LOW` ($R < 0.35$). Each incident dossier is sealed with a deterministic SHA-256 evidence digest.

\subsection{Cross-Cutting Layer: Post-Quantum Cryptography & RL Defense}
1. \textbf{NIST FIPS 203 ML-KEM-768}: Key establishment utilizes module-lattice key encapsulation. A 32-byte shared secret is derived via HKDF-SHA256 and paired with AES-256-GCM authenticated encryption for data-plane traffic.
2. \textbf{Defensive RL Mitigation}: Formulated as an un-contaminated Markov Decision Process $(\mathcal{S}, \mathcal{A}, \mathcal{P}, \mathcal{R}, \gamma)$ where action $a \in \{\text{ALLOW}, \text{ALERT}, \text{RATE\_LIMIT}, \text{QUARANTINE/BLOCK}\}$. Deep Q-Networks (DQN) optimize the Bellman equation using experience replay and target networks.
3. \textbf{Tamper-Evident Hash-Chain Audit Log}: Local audit logs maintain sequence integrity via chained hashes: $h_i = \text{SHA-256}(h_{i-1} \parallel r_i \parallel \text{seq}_i)$.

\section{Discussion, Limitations, and Future Work}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Operational Trade-Offs & Findings}
1. \textbf{Recurrent vs. Non-Recurrent Autoencoders}: Our findings confirm Mazinani et al. \cite{mazinani2026} that recurrent architectures on tabular NetFlow features add latency without boosting accuracy. Dense Autoencoders should be favored for resource-constrained IoT edge devices.
2. \textbf{False Alarm Trade-Offs}: In stationary traffic, static 95th thresholds yield slightly lower FPR ($4.93\%$) than dynamic tracking ($5.01\%$). However, dynamic controllers prevent catastrophic failure when traffic rates surge.
3. \textbf{Alert Fatigue Elimination}: Converting 74,453 raw flow alerts into 91 multi-stage incidents bridges the operational gap between theoretical anomaly detectors and frontline SOC triage.

\subsection{Limitations}
1. \textbf{Tabular Flows vs. Raw Payloads}: CICIoT2023 provides 39 aggregated flow statistics rather than raw packet payloads. Deep packet inspection (DPI) of application payload byte content was not evaluated.
2. \textbf{Host Hardware Scope}: Benchmarks were executed on an x86\_64 edge server architecture; physical microcontroller deployment (e.g., Cortex-M4/M7) represents an objective for future hardware-in-the-loop studies.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
This study presented DACC-ID, a drift-aware, contextual cybersecurity incident detection and correlation framework resolving the key limitations identified in Saeed \cite{saeed2025}. Through rigorous 5-seed evaluation on 100,000 held-out flows of CICIoT2023, DACC-ID demonstrates that: (1) Sample-level Dense Autoencoders match LSTM accuracy while slashing latency by $72.7\%$ and parameters by $91.7\%$; (2) Truncation-bias corrected thresholding eliminates EWMA collapse and slashes drift false alarms by up to $77\%$; (3) Temporal graph correlation achieves a $99.88\%$ alert reduction rate, transforming tens of thousands of isolated alerts into prioritized, MITRE ATT\&CK-mapped incidents; and (4) Standardized NIST FIPS 203 ML-KEM-768 and tamper-evident hash chaining provide sub-millisecond post-quantum confidentiality and forensic integrity.
